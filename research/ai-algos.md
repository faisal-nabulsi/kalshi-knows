# AI and algorithmic pick sites, and the in-play latency world (Round 1, verified in Round 2)

Written Oct 11 2026 for the Kalshi Knows hackathon. Plain-language report. Every outside fact has a link; our own numbers (7.0 s, 38.6/50.4/64.8 s, 1,596 fires) come from our repo and are labelled "ours". Items I could not confirm are flagged "unverified".

Jargon, defined once:
- **Closing line**: the final sportsbook price just before kickoff. Widely treated as the best public estimate of a game's true odds.
- **ATS (against the spread)**: a pick that picks a side of the point spread. At standard -110 pricing you must win about 52.4% to break even (my arithmetic: 110/210).
- **Vig / overround**: the sportsbook's built-in margin. **No-vig / "fair" odds** = prices with the margin removed.
- **+EV (positive expected value)**: a bet whose price is better than the fair price. It can still lose any single time.
- **Brier score**: average squared error of probability forecasts (lower is better).
- **Latency**: delay between a real event and when a system learns of or shows it.

## Executive summary

1. **Most "AI pick" products are one of four things**: Monte Carlo simulation of games (BetQL, Dimers, SportsLine), machine-learning margin/total models (Dimers 2026 rebuild, Rithmm), market-derived "+EV" scanners that copy sharp-book prices (OddsJam-style tools), or LLM forecasters (research stage). Their accuracy claims are almost all self-reported.
2. **Public accuracy claims are near break-even.** Dimers reports 52.7% ATS for 2025, only about 0.3 points above the 52.4% break-even. I found no independent audit. Independent tracking of tipsters finds most lose money (see Section 6).
3. **The honest benchmark is the closing line, and it is hard to beat.** Open NFL models track it closely but slightly underperform it; research finds NFL lines mostly efficient with small, unstable biases.
4. **The only systematic edge in the market is speed or price, not prediction**: +EV scanners and latency arbitrage. Nobody can credibly claim to out-predict the market on pregame NFL sides.
5. **In-play latency is real and documented.** Super Bowl LX streams ran about 48 to 62 s behind the field versus about 19 s for broadcast TV. Official data feeds are fast and sold to sportsbooks; the gap viewers face is the stream delay.
6. **Where we sit**: our approach (infer the play from Kalshi order-flow, then measure market vs ESPN vs stream) is a fan-facing "spoiler" product. I found no product doing exactly this, and no published study measuring Kalshi in-game price timing against broadcast. Closest work is academic (real-time prediction-market updating) and the PolySwarm paper's latency-arbitrage module. Absence of evidence is not proof, so say "we found none" in the pitch, not "nobody".

## 3. Market-implied "+EV" tools (OddsJam, Outlier, Unabated, Crazy Ninja)

How they work: pull prices from a sharp book (usually Pinnacle) or a market average, strip the vig to get fair odds, and flag any sportsbook price better than fair ([RotoWire OddsJam review](https://www.rotowire.com/news/best-betting-strategy-for-the-long-term-get-a-7-day-free-trial-for-sharp-money-97444)). Pinnacle's 1 to 3% margin makes it the usual reference ([OddsPapi](https://oddspapi.io/blog/?p=3089)). The devig method matters on longshots: proportional, Shin and power methods differ by about 9% on a lopsided total ([OddsPapi](https://oddspapi.io/blog/?p=2906)).

Takeaways: this is "copy the market" arbitrage between books, not forecasting. Speed matters because opportunities vanish in seconds ([RotoWire](https://www.rotowire.com/news/best-betting-strategy-for-the-long-term-get-a-7-day-free-trial-for-sharp-money-97444)). I did not retrieve official pages for Unabated, Outlier or Crazy Ninja Odds, so their specific methods and prices are unverified. Third-party API pricing for no-vig data reaches $229/mo on one vendor ([OddsPapi](https://oddspapi.io/blog/?p=2906)). Unabated is reported at about $3,000/mo sales-gated by competitor comparison sites, with conflicting $99-199/mo consumer estimates (unverified); OddsJam and Outlier prices are not published ([sportsapis.dev](https://sportsapis.dev/apis/unabated)).

## 4. Prediction-market and LLM forecasting

- **ForecastBench**: by July 2026 the Forecasting Research Institute said AI models had likely reached parity with superforecasters, but cautioned that the human numbers date from 2024 and are extrapolated; the evidence is thin (reported one-sided p-values 0.41 for the top system, 0.14-0.16 for others, overlapping intervals) and Good Judgment argues superforecasters still lead the full board, especially on market questions (80.3 vs 75.8) ([FRI](https://forecastingresearch.substack.com/p/ai-models-have-likely-reached-parity)). An August 2026 critique disputes the market-question result ([EA Forum](https://forum.effectivealtruism.org/posts/8YuG9EuhyYqaNBt7F/the-copier-line-what-forecastbench-s-market-scores-actually)). Earlier: best model Brier 0.101 vs superforecasters 0.081 ([FRI](https://forecastingresearch.substack.com/p/llms-are-closing-the-gap-on-human)). This measures calibrated long-range questions, not play-by-play sports.
- **PredictionMarketBench**: backtests trading agents on Kalshi data (4 episodes: crypto, weather, sports) with fee-aware simulation; naive agents lose to fees and settlement losses ([arXiv 2602.00133](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.00133)). Relevant to our fee-aware Dutch Book finding (0 arbitrages after fees).
- **PolySwarm** (Barot and Borkhatariya, April 2026; academic paper, not a product I could find): 50 LLM personas vote on Polymarket probabilities, sized with quarter-Kelly, plus a latency-arbitrage module using crypto-exchange prices to catch stale Polymarket quotes; authors flag hallucination and cost ([alphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2604.03888)). Live performance unverified; the abstract reports only better Brier/calibration than single-model baselines and no trading returns.
- Other agent tools (Swarms, PolyClaw, a Polymarket MCP server) exist as open tooling ([Swarms docs](https://docs.swarms.world/examples/finance/prediction-markets-polymarket)).
- **Kalshi vs sportsbooks pregame**: a 2026 CMC senior thesis (Dylan Cotti) found Kalshi slightly more accurate than DraftKings on pregame win probabilities, marginally significant, with the edge mostly vanishing after controlling for how each platform distributes probabilities ([Claremont](https://scholarship.claremont.edu/cmc_theses/4214)). RotoWire found Kalshi's pricing beat FanDuel/DraftKings in weeks 1 and 2 of the 2026 season ([RotoWire](https://www.rotowire.com/article/kalshi-beats-draftkings-fanduel-on-nfl-week-2-pricing-134248)), while Citizens (Dec 26 2025, Week 17, 26 data points) found Kalshi pregame odds 8% worse than FanDuel and 6% worse than DraftKings including fees ([CDC Gaming](https://cdcgaming.com/brief/kalshi-pricing-remains-worse-than-sportsbooks-says-citizens/)). The Casino.org page cited in Round 1 does not say this (it covers Week 3 of 2025). Citizens later reported Kalshi beating both books in 2026 Week 1, and RotoWire's Week 3 shows near parity (vig Kalshi 4.38%, FanDuel 4.37%, DraftKings 4.52%). Mixed and moving.

## 5. In-play and latency world

- **Official data**: Genius Sports is the NFL's exclusive official betting-data distributor (live play data and Next Gen Stats), after taking it from Sportradar in 2021; the 2023 extension ran through 2027-28 and the June 2025 extension runs through the 2029 season (Super Bowl 2030), so the sources do not truly conflict and 2030 is current ([Covers](https://www.covers.com/industry/nfl-genius-sports-announce-extension-with-multi-year-deal-june-12-2025); [National Football Post](https://www.nationalfootballpost.com/nfl-extends-expands-exclusive-data-deal-with-genius-sports)); the 2027-28 figure comes from the older Legal Sports Report article. Genius says it powers over 98% of legal US NFL betting data ([same](https://www.legalsportsreport.com/123307/genius-extends-nfl-betting-data-deal-at-least-five-years/)). No public latency spec exists for either vendor; a law-firm note describes the gap to TV as "a few seconds", a generic estimate ([Lewis Silkin](https://sportsinsights.lewissilkin.com/post/102i00o/sports-data-in-the-spotlight-genius-sports-and-sportradar-settle-one-of-the-high)). Faster feeds are tiered and priced: in soccer the 2022 settlement gave Genius the low-latency feed and Sportradar a delayed one ([Gambling Insider](https://www.gamblinginsider.com/news/18587/genius-sports-and-sportradar-litigation-dispute-resolved)).
- **Courtsiding**: people at the venue relay results before operators update odds. The UK Gambling Commission says it is not cheating under the Gambling Act but may breach event terms ([UKGC](https://www.gamblingcommission.gov.uk/licensees-and-businesses/guide/in-play-or-in-running-betting)); an AP report says even a few seconds of edge matters ([Huck](https://huckmag.com/article/inside-the-murky-world-of-tennis-courtsiding)).
- **Market reaction speed**: Croxson and Reade (Economic Journal, 2014) studied goals just before half-time and concluded betting prices "update swiftly and fully" ([Birmingham](https://research.birmingham.ac.uk/en/publications/information-and-efficiency-goal-arrival-in-soccer-betting/)). I could not confirm the data source (Betfair is often assumed; unverified) or any seconds figure. Other timing evidence: a 2026 arXiv paper reportedly found 99% of Betfair Exchange reactions to 470 goals absorbed within two minutes ([arXiv 2605.16066](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.16066); from a search summary, not confirmed in the PDF), and a 2003 ACM paper measured about 31.6 s average goal-to-price delay on 74 goals ([ACM 2003](https://clgiles.ist.psu.edu/papers/ACM-2003-Sports-Betting.pdf); dated); the full paper is at the [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1111/ecoj.12033). A related 2026 paper finds real-time prediction-market prices respond quickly but incompletely, about 0.64-for-one per minute, with leftover drift for several minutes, worse in thin markets ([arXiv 2606.07811](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.07811)). That paper (Angelini and De Angelis, June 5 2026) does not name the platform in its abstract (one search summary said Kalshi; unverified) and gives no second-level numbers.
- **Stream delay (Super Bowl LX, Stats Perform)**: broadcast TV about 19 s behind, cable 38 s, Peacock 48 s, YouTube TV and Hulu Live 53 s, DirecTV Stream 60 s, NFL+ 62 s; YouTube TV viewers varied by 126 s ([Stats Perform via SVG](https://www.sportsvideo.org/2026/02/11/stats-perform-2026-super-bowl-latency-report/); [CSI](https://csimagazine.com/csi/Peacock-leads-SuperBowlLX-streaming-latency.php)). Our "19 to 62 s" range matches this.
- **Ours (not external)**: Kalshi moves a median 7.0 s after ESPN's play timestamp (417 big plays); our alerts beat ESPN's feed by 38.6/50.4/64.8 s.
- **Kalshi context**: the WSJ describes Kalshi traders trading during live games and exiting within 45 seconds, and notes Kalshi does not suspend trading like sportsbooks ([Rotowire/WSJ summary](https://www.rotowire.com/article/kalshi-beats-draftkings-fanduel-on-nfl-week-1-pricing-134248) and [WSJ via Tiger](https://www.itiger.com/hans/news/2570847487)); I could not independently open the WSJ text. Kalshi also has a FOX partnership ([Barron's via Tiger](https://www.itiger.com/hans/news/2589322998)).

## 6. What edge is realistic vs marketing

- **Marketing**: season-long hit rates of 52 to 54% ATS are indistinguishable from break-even after vig. Single hot weeks (13-3) are noise ([Dimers recap](https://www.dimers.com/nfl/news/nfl-week-4-betting-recap-where-the-dimers-model-dominated-and-why-it-pays-to-follow)).
- **Independent evidence on picks**: a 2026 preprint (Makinde, Onasanya, Adelakun, "Evidence from the Nigerian Market", about $4.8M in tracked bets) tracked 5,467 pre-match slips from three X/Telegram tipsters, verified against Stake.com; tipsters lost 25.24% and a follower staking evenly lost 38.27% ([Emergent Mind summary of arXiv 2604.08251](https://www.emergentmind.com/papers/2604.08251)). Single market, three tipsters, preliminary. Tips.GG reports only 38 of 165 football (soccer, not NFL) prediction services (23%) were profitable Jan to Aug 2026 at flat $10 stakes; the most accurate service (76.0% win rate) still had ROI -1.94%, only 4 of 73 services with 1,000+ picks profited, and Tips.GG's own picks were -3.96% ([Coincub](https://coincub.com/?p=14530)); Tips.GG ranks tipsters itself, so check independence. Win rate and ROI diverge: favorite-pickers win often and still lose.
- **NFL market efficiency**: studies find closing lines fairly efficient with small, unstable biases such as favorite over-betting ([Tuttle et al.](https://journalofeconomicinsight.com/index.php/joei/article/view/1335); [Miller and Rapach](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/empfin/v24y2013icp10-23.html)). A 2017 analysis found only 2 of 33 models that picked NFL games ATS for ten seasons would have profited ([nfelo summary](https://nfeloapp.com/games/nfl-model-performance/)); treat as indirect.
- **Realistic edges**: (a) price shopping and +EV vs sharp books, (b) speed (latency), (c) liquidity and fee awareness. Predictive "AI" on pregame sides is the least credible.

## 7. Where our approach sits

- Our model family (XGBoost on nflverse play-by-play) is the same one the open-source community uses for EP and WP, so it is defensible and reproducible ([fastrmodels](https://nflverse.r-universe.dev/api/packages/fastrmodels)).
- Our twist is **inference from order flow**, not predicting outcomes: classify what just happened from the market's reaction, then alert before the stream. This is a latency product, not a pick product, which sidesteps the "52% ATS" credibility problem.
- Nearest neighbors: PolySwarm's latency arbitrage (crypto-to-Polymarket, not sports plays), Kalshi live traders, and courtsiding (human spotters). I found no sports "spoiler" product built on prediction-market flow, and no published Kalshi live-game vs broadcast timing study. Say "we could not find one".
- Honesty points for judges: (1) our 7.0 s market lag is median over big plays only; (2) beating ESPN's feed does not mean beating sportsbook feeds, which are faster and paid; (3) Dutch Book result (0 after fees) is consistent with fee-aware backtests like PredictionMarketBench; (4) the 2026 Kalshi pricing evidence is mixed, so we should not claim Kalshi is "sharper".
